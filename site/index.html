Why passwords and MFA don't help

Consent phishing doesn't steal a password. It asks Google, politely, to hand an app access to your account, and someone on your team says yes.

The consent screen is real

It's Google's own page, so it looks exactly as it should. The only clue is a list of permissions that nobody reads.

Access outlives the click

A grant keeps working until someone revokes it: weeks or months later, if anyone notices at all.

The free controls are hidden

Google's app controls are admin-only and say nothing at the moment someone clicks. Enterprise tools start at $750/month.

How it works

grantgate sits in your browser, between the “Sign in with Google” button and Google's consent screen.

1

Catches the sign-in

It spots Google's sign-in request in your browser before it reaches Google.

2

Explains it plainly

Every permission the app asks for gets a plain-English label and a high, medium or low rating.

3

Holds back the risky ones

High-risk requests wait until you confirm, unless the app is on your allow list. Apps you block never get through.

4

Keeps a private record

Every sign-in goes into an encrypted ledger that only your passphrase opens. Export it as CSV, and share your lists with staff as a policy file.

Three risk levels, in plain English

grantgate rates each permission an app asks for. Here are some it knows, in its own words: “This app wants to…”

High risk

Stopped until you confirm, or until your allow list says the app is OK.

  • read, send, delete and manage all your email
  • see, edit, create and delete all your Google Drive files
  • create, change and delete user accounts in your organization

Medium risk

Allowed, and written to your ledger so you can review it later.

  • see, edit, share and permanently delete all your calendars
  • see and download your contacts

Low risk

Goes straight through: this is all an ordinary sign-in asks for.

  • confirm who you are when you sign in
  • see your email address
  • see your name and profile picture

Private by design

The extension has no server. Your record of sign-ins and your allow and block lists stay in your browser, and the record is encrypted with a passphrase only you know. grantgate never sees your Google account, your tokens or your email.
